# Format "What If... Real Life" — game characters dropped into the mundane real world

> TODO(style-refactor): style must come from the resolved chain (story bible top-level `style_tags` → game config `prompt_prefix`); character cards are style-free. This file not yet audited for hardcoded house-style.

A recognizable game character is placed into a banal real-world situation or job — the contrast between who they ARE (their game persona, abilities, mannerisms, way of speaking) and who they have to BE in this context generates the comedy. Examples: Spock as a coffee barista, Kratos as a kindergarten teacher, a blocky Roblox guy trying to shop at a luxury boutique, Master Chief at a DMV counter, Geralt as a vet's receptionist.

This file is the **playbook**: when to use, concept, budget, role briefs. Craft rules (TTS, NB2 chain, H3 I2V and H3 lip-sync, ffmpeg) — in `rules/roles/*.md`.

---

## When to use

Keywords: "what if", "real life", "real world", "real-world", "что если", "в реальной жизни", "реальная жизнь", "fish out of water", "cameo", "mundane job", "ordinary life".

Best fit when the game has a strongly characterized hero (Spock, Kratos, Master Chief, Geralt, an iconic Roblox/MOBA/MMO avatar) whose persona is so specific that putting them in any ordinary context generates instant absurdity. **Single-shot premise per video**, but a format-local story bible is mandatory for locked style, appearance, voice routing, and anti-repetition.

Resolve `STORY_BIBLE_PATH` to `games/{GAME_TAG}/what-if-real-life_story_bible.json` before production. If it does not exist, ask the user whether to create it and STOP until they confirm. Never read appearance cards, images, or voice samples from another format unless the user explicitly tells you to copy a specific item. Update this bible only after the user explicitly approves the presented final video.

## Concept

### The contrast rule

The character's reaction is **internally consistent with the game persona** (Spock is logical, Kratos is gruff/angry, a Roblox blocky guy is naively literal, Master Chief is military-formal) but **mismatched to the real-world context**. The contrast IS the joke; reactions don't have to be deadpan — they have to be in-character for a place where being in-character is wrong.

The chosen tone is fixed for the whole video in `visual_identity.reaction_mode` and applied to every character line. Mixing tones mid-video kills the bit ("Spock suddenly enthusiastic" = no).

### Locked across the format

- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps
- **Style:** Photorealistic — even if the character is from a stylized source (Roblox block, low-poly MMO avatar). The character is rendered as if **physically present** in a real-world location: a Roblox guy is real cubic plastic in a real boutique, not a screenshot. Realism amplifies the absurd.
- **Subtitles:** one universal style (editor.md §6), generated by Editor via whisper on `build/voice-final.wav`. No BorderStyle=4/MarginV=120/mask.
- **Speedup:** 1.18× at the end (snappier than the standard 1.15× — comedy timing). Applied BEFORE music mix. See `rules/roles/editor.md` §14.
- **Music:** Generated via MiniMax with an upbeat, light `style` prompt (instrumental) — see `rules/roles/sound.md` §6. Volume 0.08. Never dark/dramatic — a somber underscore will tank the mood. See `rules/roles/sound.md` §11 (music tone).
- **Models — all-H3:** action scenes run as plain I2V on **MiniMax H3 Max** (route `minimax.h3max.i2v`); dialogue scenes run as **H3 lip-sync** on **MiniMax H3 Max Turbo** (route `minimax.h3max.turbo.i2v` plus `target_audio_url` = the scene's TTS; `rules/roles/animator.md` §2a "H3 lip-sync"). These two routes side by side are the one allowed exception to one route per episode; a plain clip never runs on Turbo. No Kling Avatar clips. Still shots only as allowed below.
- **Artifact guard — HARDENED (replaces the removed artifact mask; formerly the Avatar negative prompt):** `extra characters facing camera, lipsync on background extras, CJK text, Chinese characters, Japanese characters, Korean characters, hanzi, kanji, hangul, garbled text, gibberish glyphs, on-screen captions, bottom-bar text, UI overlay, station ID, ticker text, signage with text, any text at frame bottom, subtitle text in scene, watermark`
- **H3 prompts:** H3 has no `negative_prompt`. In I2V (action) prompts the hardened list becomes at most two short plain closing sentences, e.g. `No on-screen text, captions or signage text.`; physical gags also end with `No music, no voices.` Lip-sync (dialogue) prompts end with the lip-sync sentence instead and need no sound clause (Animator brief).

### Still shots: allowed

The scenarist may plan still shots (`motion: "still"`) under the rule, cap and fields in `rules/roles/scenarist.md` "Still shots" (§8). A still scene gets its start PNG as usual and is rendered free by the Animator (`tools/still_shot.py`, no paid clip).

- **Best candidate:** the title card — its NB2 still gets a slow `push-in`/`pull-out` instead of an optional paid I2V clip; the four `title` overlays are drawn on top as usual.
- **Never a still:** any `dialogue-offscreen` or `dialogue-on-screen` scene (H3 lip-sync), an `inner-thought` scene (the character's face carries it), a `physical-gag` scene (the motion is the joke), or a `monologue` scene whose point is the character's silent action. Zero stills is a normal result.

### Variable per video

- Character (must be visually recognizable in 1 second)
- Real-world location/role (barista / kindergarten teacher / DMV clerk / vet receptionist / wedding photographer / boutique shopper / dentist / Uber driver / yoga instructor)
- Outfit overlay anchoring the role (apron / lanyard / polo shirt / scrubs)
- `reaction_mode` (see Reaction Modes section)
- 3–6 vignette premises
- Bg-music track generated via MiniMax with an upbeat/light `style` prompt

## Reaction modes

Pick ONE and stick with it for all character lines in the video.

| Mode | Voice direction | Example line in a barista scene |
|---|---|---|
| `deadpan-logical` | flat, slow, factual; no emotion | "This is not a beverage. This is a logical crime." |
| `gruff-angry` | gravelly, loud, impatient; treats trivial things as battles | "BOY! The almond milk is on the SHELF." |
| `naive-literal` | innocent, slow, questions obvious things | "Why does the cup have a HOLE on top? Will the coffee not fall out?" |
| `military-formal` | clipped, procedural, mission-style | "Order received. Latte engagement in five minutes. Confirm sugar payload." |
| `world-weary-monotone` | tired, low-pitch, treats every request as one more burden | "Of course you want oat milk. Everyone wants oat milk." |
| `over-the-top-heroic` | declarative, epic phrasing for trivial actions | "I shall pour the coffee. AND ALL WHO ORDER IT SHALL DRINK." |

Fix the mode in `storyboard.json → visual_identity.reaction_mode`. Sound uses it to direct the resolved speech route; Scenarist uses it to write all character lines in one register; Animator shapes the lip-sync prompt's behaviour before, during and after the line accordingly.

## Episode / video structure (flexible 5–8 scenes)

| Slot | Mandatory? | Dur. (pre-speed) | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title card | yes | 3s | Hook — "What if... / [CHARACTER] / Becomes / [ROLE]" in 4 stacked lines |
| Vignette 1 | yes | 5s | First clash between persona and reality |
| Vignette 2 | yes | 5s | Different gag mechanic, different angle |
| Vignette 3 | yes | 5s | Different gag mechanic again |
| Vignette 4 | optional | 5s | — |
| Vignette 5 | optional | 5s | — |
| Vignette 6 | optional | 5s | — |
| CTA | yes | 3s | Channel CTA from Supabase |

Total target: 28–34s pre-speedup → ~24–29s after 1.18× speedup. Hard cap 32s post-speedup (short-form sweet spot).

**Anti-repetition:** no two vignettes may use the same gag mechanic (e.g. don't have two "customer asks for absurd order" gags in a row).

## Vignette scene types

Each vignette picks ONE of these. Sound, Animator, Editor read `scene.scene_type` and dispatch accordingly.

| `scene_type` | Audio source | Animation model | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dialogue-offscreen` (default) | `customer.mp3` (off-screen voice) + silence-padded character TTS (`scene-K.mp3`, the lip-sync audio) | H3 Max Turbo lip-sync (`minimax.h3max.turbo.i2v` + `target_audio_url`) — only the character is on screen | Cheapest dialogue option. Customer face never to camera (back-of-head, off-frame, or blurred bg). See "Recommended dialogue pattern" below. |
| `dialogue-on-screen` | Two speech tracks; both characters lip-synced | 2× H3 lip-sync (one clip per speaker) — both faces visible | Doubles cost. Use sparingly, max 1 per video. Untested on H3. |
| `monologue` | Narrator VO TTS over silent visuals | H3 Max I2V (`minimax.h3max.i2v`), clip sound unused (`scene_video_audio: "none"`) + TTS overlay by Editor | Voice-of-god narration over the character doing something silly silently. |
| `inner-thought` | Character TTS over silent I2V (the persona THINKS, doesn't speak — face stays still) | H3 Max I2V (`minimax.h3max.i2v`), clip sound unused (`scene_video_audio: "none"`) + TTS overlay | "Subtitled internal monologue" — character on screen, eyes communicate, voice is internal. |
| `physical-gag` | SFX only, no speech | H3 Max I2V (`minimax.h3max.i2v`), own sound kept (`scene_video_audio: "native"`) | Kratos crushes a juice box trying to open it. Sound = the clip's own SFX. |

## Recommended dialogue pattern (`dialogue-offscreen` scenes)

This is the signature mechanic of the format — it's cheaper than two on-screen lip-sync clips and it works.

For a scene where the customer/extra speaks first and the character reacts:

```
character_lipsync_audio_mp3 = silence(customer_dur + 0.6s) + actual_tts(character_line)
```

This silence-padded mp3 is the H3 lip-sync `target_audio_url` (Sound uploads it as the scene's `tts_url`), and H3 returns it unchanged as the clip's soundtrack. **The prompt must keep the lips closed during the leading silence** (`lips closed while the off-screen customer speaks.`; Kling Avatar v2 did this on its own); with that prompt the trial clips were judged on par with Kling Avatar v2 (Tests 4/4b). Editor then mixes `customer.mp3` over the silence portion via `amix`, producing the natural rhythm "customer speaks → character pauses → character replies" with only ONE lip-sync clip per scene.

References:
- Off-screen voice mix protocol: `rules/roles/sound.md` §9
- Audio amix + RMS sanity check: `rules/roles/editor.md` §12
- Subtitles — one universal whisper-timed style: `rules/roles/editor.md` §6

## Role briefs

### Scenarist

- Pick the character (must be visually unmistakable) + real-world role + ONE `reaction_mode` from the table above. Lock it in `visual_identity`.
- Write all character lines in the chosen `reaction_mode`. No mode-flips. Sarcasm is forbidden for `deadpan-logical` and `world-weary-monotone` — those modes work because the character is sincere.
- For each vignette, pick `scene_type` from the table. Default to `dialogue-offscreen`. Allow at most one `dialogue-on-screen` per video (cost).
- Each vignette = one specific real-world element that conflicts with the persona (the espresso machine for Spock, snack-time for Kratos, the boutique price-tag system for Roblox guy). No two vignettes with the same conflict mechanic.
- General script rules — `rules/roles/scenarist.md`.

### Storyboarder

- Photorealistic NB2 across all scenes — even when the character originates from a stylized source. Render the character as a tangible physical presence in the real-world location.
- Character outfit must include a clearly visible role-overlay (apron, lanyard, scrubs) — viewers register the role in <1s.
- For `dialogue-offscreen` scenes: customers are back-to-camera, edge-of-frame, or blurred in the background. Prompt this explicitly (e.g. "customer in foreground, BACK to camera, head turned away, only shoulders visible"). Otherwise NB2 invents inconsistent faces.
- For `dialogue-on-screen` scenes: both characters in frame, framed for 2× H3 lip-sync (clean view of both mouths).
- Chain-reference using the character sheet as `anchor`, prev-scene as second ref. Location stays consistent across all vignettes of the same video.
- General rules — `rules/roles/storyboarder.md`.

### Animator

- Every animated clip runs through `tools/kling_runner.py` on H3: action clips on H3 Max `minimax.h3max.i2v`, lip-sync dialogue clips on H3 Max Turbo `minimax.h3max.turbo.i2v` (never a plain clip on Turbo). Dispatch per `scene_type`:
  - `dialogue-offscreen` → **H3 lip-sync** on `minimax.h3max.turbo.i2v` (`rules/roles/animator.md` §2a "H3 lip-sync"): `target_audio_url` = the scene's `tts_url` (the silence-padded mp3; do NOT trim the leading silence — it's the pause that makes the bit work), `duration` = its length rounded up to 0.1 s, `expect.audio: "required"`; write `scene_video_audio: "native"` and `scene_video_lipsync: true`. Before the job, run the Avatar pre-flight curl check on `tts_url` and `scene_image_url` (both 200; otherwise STOP).
  - Lip-sync prompt: build it from `facial_expression` / `action_description` with this format's dialogue rules — only the character speaks (e.g. `Only the barista speaks; the customer stays silent.`), lips closed and still in the opening silence, no gaze or head lock (`unblinking`, `eyes locked on`, `eyes steady on`, `facing the camera head-on`, `holds still`). Add `He/She does not stare into the lens: his/her gaze rests on the off-screen customer just beside the camera and drifts naturally between phrases, with small, relaxed head movements throughout.` and end with `<Character> speaks the line "<character_line>" with lips in sync with the audio; lips closed while the off-screen customer speaks.` ≤512 UTF-8 bytes; no `No music, no voices.` clause (the soundtrack is the TTS).
  - `dialogue-on-screen` → 2× H3 lip-sync on `minimax.h3max.turbo.i2v`, one clip per speaker, each with its own padded mp3 so the two voices don't talk over each other; each prompt names its speaker and keeps the other character's lips closed while they listen. Untested on H3: flag it in the report. Do not switch model/route if a request fails.
  - `monologue`, `inner-thought` and an animated (non-still) `title-card` → H3 Max I2V (`minimax.h3max.i2v`); `expect.audio: "optional"` (never `"forbidden"`), write `scene_video_audio: "none"`, and add a **silent `target_audio_url`** of the clip's `duration` (a data URI from `tools/silent_audio.py --duration <duration_sec> --out <file>`, read in with `jq --rawfile` before `--preflight`; `rules/roles/animator.md` §2a "Silent `target_audio_url`"; it stops H3 from mouthing words, Test 6b).
  - `physical-gag` → H3 Max I2V (`minimax.h3max.i2v`) for SFX; `expect.audio: "required"`, write `scene_video_audio: "native"`, and end the prompt with `No music, no voices.`
  - H3 payload: `prompt`, `image_url`, numeric `duration` = `duration_sec`, `resolution: "768P"`, `prompt_expansion_mode: "disabled"` (see `tools/examples/minimax-h3-max-scene-video.job.json`), plus the silent `target_audio_url` on the scenes above; lip-sync clips (H3 Max Turbo) add `target_audio_url` and take the TTS-based `duration` (see `tools/examples/minimax-h3-max-turbo-lipsync.job.json`). Keep prompts ≤512 UTF-8 bytes.
  - Still scenes (`motion: "still"`, normally only the title card): no paid job; render with `tools/still_shot.py` as `rules/roles/animator.md` describes (`scene_video_audio: "none"`).
- H3 has no negative prompt: action clips end with the short closing sentences (see "Locked across the format"); lip-sync clips end with the lip-sync sentence. Generated media receives technical validation only; visual judgment belongs to the user during debug review.
- A failed generation gets one retry with the same model/settings. A second failure stops the video and returns the error; no provider, route (e.g. Kling I2V or Kling Avatar) or local fallback.
- **Debug mode:** before launching the paid H3 batch (lip-sync and I2V clips), list planned clips with per-clip cost and get user OK; a 5 s H3 Max action clip (~$0.41) is above the $0.30 confirm threshold (see `rules/roles/manager.md` Debug-mode safety rules).
- General rules — `rules/roles/animator.md`.

### Sound

- Generate character, customer, and narrator speech in this order: intentional native model audio → Chatterbox only with an approved sample owned by this resolved `what-if-real-life` bible → otherwise MiniMax.
- `reaction_mode` controls delivery direction, not a hardcoded ElevenLabs identity. New MiniMax presets or Chatterbox sample URLs go into run-local `proposed_bible_updates`; Manager stores them in this format's exact bible only after explicit final user approval. Never import them from another format.
- On a failed speech request, retry once with the same route/settings, then STOP and report the error; do not switch provider or use a local fallback.
- For each `dialogue-offscreen` scene: produce `customer.mp3` AND the silence-padded `scene-K.mp3` for H3 lip-sync, and upload `scene-K.mp3` to Supabase as the scene's `tts_url` before Phase 4 (as for Avatar): the Animator passes it as `target_audio_url`. A `dialogue-on-screen` scene gets one padded, uploaded track per speaker.
- For each `monologue` / `inner-thought` scene: produce a single TTS track for Editor to overlay.
- Subtitles — NOT Sound's job. Editor generates the one universal ASS via whisper (editor.md §6).
- Music: generated via MiniMax with an upbeat/light `style` prompt (instrumental, see `rules/roles/sound.md` §6), volume 0.08. Never dark or dramatic.
- General rules — `rules/roles/sound.md` (especially §9 off-screen voices); subtitles — `rules/roles/editor.md` §6.

### Editor

- Per-scene burn-in pipeline:
  - `title-card`: a GRAPHIC OVERLAY (`title` type in `scenes[].overlays[]`), rendered by `tools/overlays.py` (see `## Overlays`), NOT a subtitle. 4 lines: "What if..." / character name / "Becomes a" / role. No audio → anullsrc (also when the title card is a still shot). MANDATORY.
  - `dialogue-offscreen`: scale → amix(lip-sync clip audio, customer.mp3) **in the final video**. The H3 lip-sync clip (`scene_video_lipsync: true`, `scene_video_audio: "native"`) already carries the character's line — its soundtrack is the padded `scene-K.mp3` itself — so never lay `scene-K.mp3` over it again; only `customer.mp3` is mixed in. Both lines (character + off-screen customer) go into `voice-final.wav` and are subtitled in ONE universal style — there is no separate Customer style anymore. Burn — the shared `build/subs.ass` (editor.md §6).

    ⚠️ **voice-final.wav construction for dialogue-offscreen scenes — SEQUENTIAL, NOT amix:**
    
    The final video uses amix (customer plays during the lip-sync clip's leading silence window — that's correct). But `voice-final.wav` (fed to Whisper for subtitle timing) **must be built sequentially** — if you amix both audio streams into voice-final.wav, Whisper receives two voices simultaneously and will miss or misalign the customer lines entirely.
    
    Correct recipe per dialogue-offscreen scene (example: scene K, customer_dur = X seconds):
    ```
    # 1. Speed up customer
    ffmpeg -y -i scenes/scene-K/customer.mp3 -filter:a "atempo=1.18" build/vf_sceneK_cust.wav
    # 2. Extract Spock TTS from the lip-sync clip (its audio is the padded TTS, lag 0) — trim the silent head (customer_dur + 0.6s), speed up
    ffmpeg -y -i scenes/scene-K/scene-K.mp4 -vn -ss {X+0.6} -filter:a "atempo=1.18" build/vf_sceneK_spock.wav
    # 3. Concat sequentially → customer speech then Spock speech
    ffmpeg -y -i build/vf_sceneK_cust.wav -i build/vf_sceneK_spock.wav \
      -filter_complex "[0:a][1:a]concat=n=2:v=0:a=1[out]" -map "[out]" build/vf_sceneK.wav
    ```
    
    Result: Whisper hears customer line first → then Spock line → transcribes both → correct subtitle events for both speakers.
  - `dialogue-on-screen`: assemble shot-reverse-shot cuts of the two lip-sync clips (each keeps its own audio, no TTS laid over it) → burn ASS.
  - `monologue` / `inner-thought`: H3 I2V with its own audio ignored (`scene_video_audio: "none"` → anullsrc slot) → overlay TTS (scene-K.mp3) via amix → burn ASS with Default style timed from 0:00:00.00.
  - `physical-gag`: H3 I2V with its own SFX (`scene_video_audio: "native"`) → keep audio → no subtitles.
  - Every H3 clip (`scene_video_route` starting with `minimax.`), lip-sync clips included, follows the H3 trim: cut to `duration_sec` when longer, played whole when shorter — never Avatar's TTS trim; portrait scene clips use fill-and-crop ending in `setsar=1` (`rules/roles/editor.md`).
- bg-music.mp3 must be the track generated for this run and format. Fail loudly if it is absent; do not select a local or another-format fallback.
- Concat all scenes + CTA → `build/concat.mp4`.
- Apply **1.18× speedup** (`setpts=PTS/1.18` + `atempo=1.18`) BEFORE music mix. Self-check: `duration(final) ≈ sum(scene_durations) / 1.18`.
- Mix bg-music at volume 0.08.
- RMS sanity check on every `dialogue-offscreen` scene interval: > -30 dB. -40 dB = customer VO dropped.
- General rules — `rules/roles/editor.md` (especially §11 ffmpeg-full, §12 off-screen VO mix + customer subtitles, §13 artifact masking, §14 speedup).

## Anti-patterns

- ❌ Mixing game style with real world (Roblox-shaped Roblox guy in a Roblox-screenshot store) — defeats the realism contrast
- ❌ `reaction_mode` flips mid-video (Spock suddenly enthusiastic, Kratos suddenly gentle) — breaks the bit
- ❌ Customers facing camera in `dialogue-offscreen` scenes — they'd need lipsync, defeating the cost economy
- ❌ Two vignettes with the same gag mechanic (two "absurd customer order" gags, two "boss screams at toddlers" gags)
- ❌ Sending non-padded audio as the lip-sync `target_audio_url` when the gag needs customer-first rhythm
- ❌ Gaze- or head-locking phrases in a lip-sync prompt (`unblinking`, `eyes locked on`, `facing the camera head-on`, `holds still`) — H3 then stares into the lens
- ❌ Laying `scene-K.mp3` over an H3 lip-sync clip — its soundtrack already is that TTS, so the line plays twice
- ❌ Forgetting to mix off-screen / narrator VO into the final audio (RMS check catches it)
- ❌ Building `voice-final.wav` for dialogue-offscreen scenes via amix — customer and Spock get mixed simultaneously, Whisper misses customer lines entirely. Always use sequential concat: customer.mp3 → trimmed Spock TTS (see Editor brief above)
- ❌ Masking the CJK artifact with a subtitle box/drawbox — the mask is removed; fix it at the prompt level (the hardened list's closing sentences on H3 action clips)
- ❌ Touching the subtitle MarginV — the position is fixed by the universal style (editor.md §6)
- ❌ Music from `Dark/` or `Drama/` — mood mismatch
- ❌ Skipping the 1.18× speedup step
- ❌ Burning ASS subtitles with stock ffmpeg (lacks libass — subtitles silently dropped)
- ❌ Two ASS Dialogue events with overlapping time ranges within the same Style (text scrambles, word order looks broken)
- ❌ `-shortest` in any ffmpeg call (universal anti-pattern — use `apad`)
